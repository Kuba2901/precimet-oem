Quality control is a chain of checks that runs from the moment raw material is delivered to the moment finished parts reach the customer. Here is what the QC path looks like on a standard order, and what to ask for if your application requires documentation.

## Incoming inspection

Incoming inspection verifies:

- **Material grade and thickness**
- **Surface condition** — scratches, dents, corrosion spots; damaged material is set aside and goes through the complaint procedure

For steel parts intended for contact with food or pharmaceuticals, incoming inspection also verifies the release certificate against EN 10204 3.1 or 3.2 requirements, confirming the material meets the specific alloy standard (e.g. 1.4404 for 316L).

## In-process control

During production, quality checks happen at each critical operation:

### After laser cutting
- **Nesting verification** — confirming the cutting layout matches the approved one (material utilisation, sheet orientation if specified)
- **Dimensional spot-check** — key dimensions measured on the first part and at regular intervals during the batch
- **Edge quality** — burr height, cut surface finish, no heat-affected zone cracking

### After press brake bending
- **Angle measurement** — using a protractor or angle gauge; the first angle is verified against the drawing before the rest of the batch proceeds
- **Material compensation** — confirming the press brake operator has properly accounted for material springback

### After welding
- **Visual inspection** — weld bead profile, porosity, undercut, spatter
- **Dimensional check** — overall assembly dimensions, weld distortion within tolerance

### After assembly
- **Fit and function** — do parts assemble correctly, do bolted connections match the documentation, does the mechanism move freely
- **Cosmetic inspection** — surface finish, labelling, packaging condition

## Final inspection

Before parts are packed and shipped, final inspection verifies the complete order:

- **Quantity** — correct number of parts and assemblies
- **Documentation completeness** — all certificates, measurement reports and inspection records are present (where required)
- **Visual appearance** — no scratches, dents or contamination
- **Packaging** — parts adequately protected for transport

For standard orders, final inspection is a quick, structured process. For orders with a quality documentation package, every measurement is recorded and cross-referenced to the part and the correct drawing.

## What goes into a quality documentation package

Depending on the order, the quality package can include:

| Document | What it proves |
|---|---|
| **Mill Test Certificate (MTC)** | Material grade, chemical composition, mechanical properties |
| **Incoming inspection record** | Material was verified before production started |
| **First-article inspection report** | First part in the batch meets all drawing dimensions |
| **Dimensional measurement report** | Selected parts measured with measuring instruments, results recorded |
| **Welding procedure record** | Welding method, parameters, welder qualification (if applicable) |
| **Surface treatment record** | Coating inspection (quality, thickness) |
| **Delivery note / packing list** | Quantity, part numbers, order number |

The level of documentation depends on the order. A bracket for a machine frame may need only a delivery note. A part for the food or medical industry may require the full set.

## ISO 9001 in daily practice

ISO 9001:2015 is a system that ensures the quality control process is repeatable and documented. In daily practice, this means:

- **Documented procedures** — every inspection step has a defined method, frequency and acceptance criteria
- **Calibrated instruments** — measuring tools are regularly calibrated
- **Non-conformance reporting** — when a part fails inspection, there is a defined process: root-cause analysis, corrective action, re-inspection
- **Continuous improvement** — defect trends are tracked and influence process adjustments

For the customer, ISO 9001 means consistency: part #1 and part #1,000 go through the same checks.
