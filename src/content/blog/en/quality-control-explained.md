A quality certificate on the wall does not make parts correct. Quality control is a chain of checks that runs from the moment raw material enters the factory to the moment finished parts leave on the delivery truck. Here is what that chain looks like on a typical subcontracted order — and what to ask for if your application demands documentation.

## Incoming inspection

Before any laser or press brake touches the material, it gets checked. Incoming inspection verifies:

- **Material grade and thickness** — against the Mill Test Certificate (MTC) supplied with the delivery. If the drawing calls for 304L stainless at 2.0 mm, we confirm it is 304L at 2.0 mm — not 301, not 1.5 mm.
- **Surface condition** — scratches, dents, corrosion spots. Damaged stock is quarantined, not loaded onto the laser.
- **Batch traceability** — each coil or sheet is logged with its heat number, supplier and delivery date. If a defect surfaces later, we can trace it back to the exact batch.

For food-grade or pharmaceutical orders, incoming inspection is more rigorous: we verify the certificate against EN 10204 3.1 or 3.2 requirements and confirm the material meets the specific alloy standard (e.g. 1.4404 for 316L).

## In-process control

Once production starts, quality checks happen at each critical operation:

### After laser cutting
- **Dimensional spot-check** — key dimensions measured on the first part and at regular intervals during the batch
- **Edge quality** — burr height, dross-free cut surface, no heat-affected zone cracking
- **Nesting verification** — confirming the nest matches the approved layout (material utilisation, grain direction if specified)

### After bending
- **Angle measurement** — using a protractor or angle gauge; first bend verified against the drawing before the rest of the batch proceeds
- **Flange dimensions** — checked against the drawing tolerance
- **Springback compensation** — confirmed that the press brake operator has compensated for material-specific springback

### After welding
- **Visual inspection** — weld bead profile, porosity, undercut, spatter
- **Penetrant testing (PT)** — for stainless steel welds where surface cracks must be detected (on request or when specified)
- **Dimensional check** — overall assembly dimensions, weld distortion within tolerance

### After assembly
- **Fit and function** — do mating parts assemble correctly? Do bolt holes align? Does the mechanism move freely?
- **Cosmetic inspection** — surface finish, labelling, packaging condition

## Final inspection

Before parts are packed and shipped, a final inspection verifies the complete order:

- **Quantity** — correct number of parts and assemblies
- **Documentation completeness** — all certificates, measurement reports and inspection records are present
- **Visual appearance** — no scratches, dents or contamination from handling
- **Packaging** — parts are protected against corrosion (VCI wrap for steel, PE wrap for aluminium) and physical damage during transport

For standard orders, final inspection is a quick but structured check. For orders with full quality documentation packages, every measurement is recorded and cross-referenced to the drawing.

## What goes into a quality documentation package

Depending on the order, the quality package can include:

| Document | What it proves |
|----------|----------------|
| **Mill Test Certificate (MTC)** | Material grade, chemical composition, mechanical properties — traceable to the heat |
| **Incoming inspection record** | Material was verified before production started |
| **First-article inspection report** | First part in the batch meets all drawing dimensions |
| **Dimensional measurement report** | Selected parts measured with calibrated instruments; results recorded |
| **Welding procedure record** | Welding method, parameters, welder qualification (if applicable) |
| **Surface treatment record** | Coating thickness, adhesion test results (if applicable) |
| **Delivery note / packing list** | Quantity, part numbers, batch traceability |

The level of documentation depends on the order. A bracket for a machine frame may need only a delivery note. A component for food processing or medical equipment may require the full set.

## ISO 9001 in daily practice

ISO 9001:2015 is not just a certificate — it is a system that makes the checks above repeatable and documented. In daily practice it means:

- **Documented procedures** — every inspection step has a defined method, frequency and acceptance criteria
- **Calibrated instruments** — measuring tools are regularly calibrated and traceable to national standards
- **Non-conformance handling** — when a part fails inspection, there is a defined process: quarantine, root-cause analysis, corrective action, re-inspection
- **Continuous improvement** — defect trends are tracked and fed back into process adjustments

For you as a customer, ISO 9001 means consistency. Part #1 and part #1,000 go through the same checks with the same criteria — not because an operator remembers, but because the system requires it.

## When to ask for extra documentation

Most subcontracted orders do not need a full quality package. But certain industries and applications do:

- **Food and beverage** — material certificates, surface finish verification, sometimes third-party inspection
- **Medical devices** — full traceability, IQ/OQ/PQ validation, dimensional CMM reports
- **Aerospace** — NADCAP or AS9100-certified processes, first-article inspection reports per AS9102
- **Energy and pressure equipment** — EN 10204 3.2 certificates, NDT reports, hydrostatic test records
