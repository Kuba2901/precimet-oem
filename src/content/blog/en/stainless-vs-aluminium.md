Material selection is one of the first decisions on any laser-cut part — and one of the most consequential. The wrong choice means higher cost, heavier assemblies, or premature failure in the field. Below is a practical comparison of the two most common materials we cut: stainless steel and aluminium.

## At a glance

| Property | Stainless steel (304 / 316L) | Aluminium (5052 / 6061) |
|----------|-----------------------------|-------------------------|
| **Density** | ~8.0 g/cm³ | ~2.7 g/cm³ (roughly one third) |
| **Tensile strength** | 515–620 MPa (304) | 210–310 MPa (6061-T6) |
| **Corrosion resistance** | Excellent (chromium oxide layer) | Good in air; poor in alkaline/chloride |
| **Relative cost per kg** | Higher | Lower |
| **Relative cost per part** | Often comparable (heavier, but raw material cheaper per kg varies) | Depends on grade and thickness |
| **Weldability** | Excellent (TIG/MIG, no shielding gas issues) | Good (TIG preferred; clean surface required) |
| **Machinability** | Moderate (work-hardens if not controlled) | Excellent (but can be gummy) |
| **Typical finishes** | Brushed, polished, bead-blasted | Anodised, brushed, mill finish |
| **Food-grade suitability** | Yes (316L especially) | Possible (anodised or coated) |

## When to choose stainless steel

**Corrosive or hygienic environments.** Stainless is the default in food processing, pharmaceutical equipment, chemical plants and coastal applications. The passive chromium oxide layer self-heals after scratching — aluminium does not.

**Structural strength at moderate weight.** If the part must carry load without adding ribs or increasing thickness, stainless steel gives roughly twice the tensile strength of aluminium at the same gauge. Enclosures, brackets and frames that take mechanical stress often favour stainless.

**High-temperature service.** Stainless retains strength well above 300 °C; aluminium softens progressively above 150 °C. Exhaust components, heat shields and parts near welds or motors belong in stainless.

**Surface finish matters.** Brushed or polished stainless gives a premium appearance for consumer-facing products, architectural fittings and food-contact surfaces. Anodised aluminium can look excellent too, but scratches reveal raw metal underneath.

## When to choose aluminium

**Weight is critical.** At roughly one-third the density of steel, aluminium dramatically reduces part weight. Robotics, automation frames, aerospace brackets and any mobile or handheld assembly benefit from the weight savings — often enough to justify the material cost.

**Heat dissipation.** Aluminium's thermal conductivity (~160–220 W/m·K vs. ~16 W/m·K for stainless) makes it the material of choice for enclosures around electronics, heat sinks and any part where thermal management matters.

**High-volume production.** Aluminium laser-cuts faster than stainless at equivalent thickness, reducing cycle time. The material is also easier to deburr and finish, which lowers downstream costs on large batches.

**Aesthetic or anodised finishes.** Anodising aluminium creates a hard, corrosion-resistant surface in a range of colours — ideal for covers, panels and decorative elements where colour branding matters.

## What about carbon steel?

Carbon steel (S235JR, S355J2, DC01) remains the most common and most economical material we cut. It is stronger than aluminium, heavier than both alternatives, and rusts without surface treatment. For indoor structural parts, brackets, frames and anything that will be powder-coated or painted, carbon steel is often the most cost-effective choice — and the one we cut most frequently.

## Practical tips for your inquiry

1. **State the grade and thickness** — not just "stainless" or "aluminium." 304 and 316L behave very differently in welding and corrosion. 5052 and 6061 have different formability after bending.
2. **Mention the service environment** — outdoor exposure, food contact, chemical wash-down, salt spray. That lets us recommend the right grade, not just the cheapest one.
3. **Consider the full process chain** — a part that will be welded, bent and then powder-coated may perform differently in aluminium than in stainless. Tell us the downstream operations so we can advise on material as a complete process, not just a laser-cutting input.
4. **If unsure, ask** — our CAD/CAM team reviews material selection on every quotation. We flag potential issues (e.g. alloy incompatibility, cracking risk during bending) before production starts.
